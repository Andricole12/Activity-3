So... What is TANAY❓

Tanay is a municipality in the province of Rizal Province known for its rich history and natural landscapes. During the Spanish colonial period, Tanay was originally a visita of Morong before becoming an independent town in 1606. The town developed under the influence of Spanish missionaries, who helped establish Christianity and built churches and settlements that shaped the community’s early growth.

It is home to the namesake Tanay–Paete dialect of Tagalog, which apart from this municipality is spoken on other towns located on the eastern shores of the Laguna Lake.

History

Early Period

Credits: https://www.facebook.com/

Tanay was first settled by early Austronesian peoples. During the Spanish period in the late 1500s, Franciscan missionaries arrived in the area after the Spanish conquest of Manila and nearby lake communities led by Juan de Salcedo.

At first, Tanay was part of Pililla, but it became an independent town in 1606 under the name “Monte de Tan-ay.”

The town experienced early challenges, including a rebellion in 1638 that destroyed the settlement, but it was rebuilt two years later at its present location.

Modern Developments

Credits: Drew Arellano's Tanay rest house

During the Spanish era, Tanay became part of the Politico-Militar Distrito de Morong together with towns such as Morong, Baras, and Angono.

The town later played a role in the Philippine Revolution, serving as the headquarters of the revolutionary government under Emilio Aguinaldo.

In the twentieth century, Tanay experienced American and Japanese occupation periods, and its mountains became a base for guerrilla fighters during World War II. In more recent history, the town continued to develop while facing major events such as the flooding caused by Typhoon Ketsana in 2009.

Culture and Traditions

Hane Festival

The Tanay Hane Festival is an annual, month-long (typically November) celebration in Tanay, Rizal, Philippines, commemorating the municipality's founding anniversary.

It showcases agri-eco-tourism, local arts, culture, and products, featuring street dancing, a people's parade, and culinary competitions, all celebrating the local, welcoming phrase "hane".
