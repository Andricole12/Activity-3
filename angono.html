So... What is ANGONO❓

Angono, officially the Municipality of Angono (Tagalog: Bayan ng Angono), is a municipality in the province of Rizal, Philippines. According to the 2024 census, it has a population of 134,975 people.



It is best known as the Art Capital of the Philippines, being the hometown of national artist for music Lucio San Pedro and national artist for visual arts Carlos "Botong" Francisco, as well as the site of the Angono Petroglyphs, the oldest known work of art in the country.

History

Early Period

Credits: https://www.facebook.com/

First created as a pueblo in 1766, Angono was a barrio of its neighboring town Taytay and Binangonan before it was legally proclaimed an independent municipality in 1938 by then President Manuel L. Quezon.

The municipality was formalized by law on June 17, 1972, through Republic Act No. 6469, which was signed by then President Ferdinand E. Marcos.

Modern Developments

Credits: Drew Arellano's Tanay rest house

The town is currently campaigning for its inclusion in the UNESCO Creative Cities Network as it is a center for visual arts.

It is also campaigning for the inclusion of the Angono Petroglyphs (1 of 5 properties of the Petroglyphs and Petrographs of the Philippines UNESCO tentative site) in the World Heritage List.

Culture and Traditions

Parehadora

Credits: parehadora-2024-kapistahan-ni-san-clemente-angono-rizal

The traditional parehadoras are group of young girls holding paddles and wearing bakya or wooden slippers and dressed in a colorful outfit which joins the procession in the feast day of Pope Clement I.

They usually march at the beat of the band playing alongside them. Now with a dying lake, the tradition of the Parehadora is a reminder that Angono was once a fishing village.

Salubong

Credits: By Ryomaandres - Own work, CC BY-SA 4.0

The Salubong in Angono, Rizal, is a renowned Easter Sunday dawn tradition reenacting the meeting of the Risen Christ and the Virgin Mary.

Held near the municipal building, it features the Galilea (a stage), the Sayaw ng Bati (dance of greeting), and a young angel descending from a giant paper heart to remove Mary's black veil.

Higantes Festival

Credits: By Ryomaandres - Own work, CC BY-SA 4.0

Angono's joyous fiesta in honor of Pope Clement I whose image, resplendent in papal vestments, is held November 22–23, and involves a procession accompanied by parehadoras devotees dressed in colorful local costumes, wooden shoes and carrying boat paddles and higantes, giant papier-mâché effigies.

The street event culminates in fluvial procession at Laguna de Bay amidst revelry that continues until the image is brought back to its sanctuary.

It was said that the higantes started during the Spanish colonial times. When Angono was once a hacienda and ruled by Spanish hacienderos The Guido. The Angono land tillers' way of protesting their struggle was by making giant effigies of their landlords whose hands are usually high up on their waist.

Delicacies

Balaw Balaw

Credits: http://alex-insights.blogspot.com/2013/09/ginisang-balaw-balaw-sauteed-balaw-balaw.html

A traditional Filipino fermented condiment or appetizer made from rice and small shrimps (alamang) or fish, mixed with angkak (red yeast rice) to create a distinct red color and acidic taste

Minaluto

Credits: https://www.taraletsanywhere.com/balaw-balaw-restaurant-and-art-gallery-rizal/

A signature Filipino rice dish from the Balaw-Balaw Restaurant in Angono, Rizal, often described as a local, rustic version of Spanish paella. Served in a large bilao (woven tray)

Kalderetang Itik

Credits: https://lalunacafe.ph/product/kalderetang-itik/

A savory, spicy stew made with local native duck.

Kumanoy

Credits:https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hIpKbTJLDQo

A traditional kakanin (rice cake) delicacy known for its soft, creamy texture similar to inutak. It is made from glutinous rice flour, sugar, and coconut milk, often topped with a rich, toasted coconut cream glaze.
