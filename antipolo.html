So... What is ANTIPOLO❓

Antipolo, officially the City of Antipolo (Filipino: Lungsod ng Antipolo), is a component city and the capital of the province of Rizal Province in the Philippines.

According to the 2024 census, it has a population of 913,712 people, making it the most populous city in Rizal Province and in the CALABARZON region, as well as the seventh most populous city in the Philippines. It is also recognized as the most populated city with component city status in the country.

History

Spanish Period

Credits: CoptEgypt136, Iglesia Parroquial de Nuestra Señora de la Anunciata

During the Spanish colonial period, Franciscan missionaries arrived in Antipolo in 1578 and built a small church in what is now Boso-Boso Church.

In 1591, Jesuit missionaries replaced them and organized the settlement into a parish as Christianity spread among the locals.

In 1626, Governor-General Juan Niño de Tabora brought the image of the Our Lady of Peace and Good Voyage from Acapulco, which became widely venerated by devotees.

American and Japanese Period

Credits: Patrickroque01, Aerial View of Antipolo

During the American and Japanese periods, Antipolo experienced political changes and hardships caused by war.

It became part of Rizal Province in 1901 and served briefly as the capital of Morong during the First Philippine Republic.

After World War II, the town rebuilt important landmarks like the Antipolo Cathedral and grew into a major pilgrimage destination.

Delicacies

Suman

https://alluringantipolo.weebly.com/delicacies.html

Suman is one of the best delicacies in Antipolo. Suman is made of rice, but it's not an ordinary rice. It's called "malagkit". Suman is one of my favorite delicacies in Antipolo especially when you put sugar on it.

Kasoy

https://alluringantipolo.weebly.com/delicacies.html

Antipolo is famous for its cashew products. You can find roasted cashews, cashew brittle, and cashew sweets sold in many stalls around the city.

Mangoes

https://alluringantipolo.weebly.com/delicacies.html

Fresh mangoes are widely sold in Antipolo, often eaten with bagoong (fermented shrimp paste) or used in desserts.

Sinukmani

https://alluringantipolo.weebly.com/delicacies.html

A sweet rice delicacy cooked with coconut milk and brown sugar, sometimes topped with latik (toasted coconut curds).
